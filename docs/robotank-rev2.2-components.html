What Δ10 changes, and why

The rigid-body simulation in the project app showed that the Rev 2.1 chassis cannot climb the first 180 mm riser from the floor. With the rear track on the floor and the nosing sliding along the belt, the centre of gravity never passes over the step edge, so the rover pitches to vertical and falls onto its back. Friction, motor torque and approach speed do not change the outcome. A 620 mm wheelbase reaches the second nosing before the rear lifts and climbs the whole flight in the same model.

  • Wheelbase 384 → 620 mm, overall 470 → 706 mm. Side plates, floor, deck and lid grow with it.
  • Track 33 → 49 links per side. 1.5 kg of TPU instead of 1 kg.
  • Four mid-run support rollers added. A 620 mm bottom run sags without them and the lugs would skip the sprocket.
  • Mass 3.07 → 4.0 kg per the sheet 4 mass column. Torque per side rises to 0.61 N·m at 35°, still 1.6× inside the motor rating. Runtime drops to about 1.3 h. No electronics change.
  • Cost ₹24,360 → ₹27,090, all of it in the drive train and frame.

Not in the total

  • 3D printer access for sprockets, idlers, rollers and 98 TPU links. Printing TPU at a service bureau costs about ₹6,000 to ₹9,000 for the track set; a direct-drive printer of your own prints it for the filament cost already counted.
  • Spot welder for the pack, about ₹2,500, or buy the pre-built pack noted in section 3.
  • Spares worth holding: 1 gearmotor (₹950), 1 BTS7960 (₹400), 1 VL53L0X (₹300), 1 support roller, 12 track links. About ₹2,200.
  • Cloud side: a free-tier MQTT broker and dashboard cover the 10 Hz telemetry; no cost at one rover.

Where the money goes and where it can move

  • Two items are 26% of the build: the gamepad and the battery pack. Neither is a place to cut. The gamepad is the safety link and the pack sets runtime and the CG.
  • Δ10 adds ₹2,730, all in the drive train and frame. The short-track alternative is a flipper mechanism, which needs two more motors, drivers and a pivot and costs more than the extra 236 mm of chassis.
  • Cheapest meaningful saving: BME280 instead of BME680 (₹950) if air-quality readings are not part of the use, and 3 mm side plates (₹500).
  • Do not substitute L298N for BTS7960, TT motors for the encoder gearmotors, or a LiPo pouch without a BMS. Each one was removed in the Rev 2.0 audit (Δ4, Δ5) for a reason on sheet 2.
  • Biggest risk to the estimate is the frame cutting quote. Get two quotes with the DXF before ordering the electronics.
